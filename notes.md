**Framework: Pipecat.** Confirmed standard, and the right call. One detail worth knowing: Pipecat now ships **Pipecat Skills for Claude Code** — it can scaffold projects, deploy to Pipecat Cloud, and more. That's directly useful to you. Also note Whisker, a real-time Pipecat debugger, and Tail, a terminal dashboard for monitoring — both feed your monitoring requirement for free.

**LLM (the persona brain).** This is where character is generated, so don't optimize for raw speed here — optimize for *instruction-following and voice consistency*. The short cryptic answers of a séance keep generation latency low regardless, so you have room. I'd run **Claude (Sonnet-tier)** as the default persona model — it holds a character voice and rhetorical constraints across a conversation better than the fast-but-flighty options, and the séance's short answers mean you won't feel the cost. Keep a fast model (Groq-hosted Llama, or Gemini Flash) wired as a swappable alternative so you can *feel* the latency/quality tradeoff yourself — that's literally your 80/20 list's "function calling under latency constraints" lesson, made tangible. Pipecat makes the LLM service a one-line swap, so build it swappable from the start.

**STT.** Pick one and go deep — that's your roadmap instruction. **Deepgram** is the pragmatic default: streaming, low latency, first-class Pipecat support, strong French. Your one wrinkle is *accents* — your séance guests have heavy ones, but remember the user talking to them is *you*, in normal French or English, so the STT only has to transcribe a clean speaker. The accents live entirely on the TTS side. That simplifies your STT choice a lot: just pick Deepgram and learn it properly.

**TTS — the load-bearing decision.** Your whole concept rests here, and the research is unambiguous about the shape of the tradeoff. Voice-generation model choice is per-shot, not per-project — but for a weekend MVP you need one vendor learned deeply, so here's the call:

Go **ElevenLabs** as your primary. The reason is specific to you: its voice library has thousands of shared voices across different ages, regions, languages and accents — a Southern cowboy, a regional British accent, and it excels at creating consistent character voices and studio-grade narration, ideal for storytelling. A séance of archetypes — a Zola laundress, a 1900 Russian émigré, a Dead Poets declaimer — *is* a character-voice casting problem, and that library is your casting pool. The cost downside is real (Cartesia is roughly one-fifth the cost across self-serve plans) but at weekend-MVP volume it's negligible.

Two things to keep in your back pocket. **Cartesia** is worth wiring as the swappable alternative — Sonic delivers time-to-first-audio of 40-90ms, purpose-built for real-time conversation — so you can demo the expressiveness-vs-latency tradeoff live, which is a great talking point. And **Hume** is the genuinely interesting wildcard for your concept: for character voices with consistent affect, Hume is the model worth evaluating first, and its EVI reads emotion in the user's voice and adjusts accordingly — a séance medium that *hears your nervousness* is a hell of a demo. **Frontend.** React, yes.
